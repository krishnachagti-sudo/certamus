About Certamus

What this page is, what goes on it, and what deliberately does not.

What this is

Certamus is the competition record of Conyso. Four people from IIM Sirmaur enter case competitions under the company's own name rather than as a team assembled for each one, and one of them enters hackathons with a collaborator from outside the institute.

The name is Latin for a contest. It is also first person: we contend.

What goes on it

Two rules decide what appears here, and both of them keep pages off.

Only finals and wins. A competition reaches this page if we reached its final round or won it. Early exits are not recorded. This is a record of results, not an activity log, and a page that listed everything would tell you nothing about whether any of it went well.

Only with the deck. A case is published when we still have the file we submitted. That sounds like a technicality and it is the more restrictive of the two rules in practice: we have reached finals whose decks nobody kept, and those are not here. What is missing from the record is usually paperwork rather than result.

The effect is a page shorter than the work behind it. We started in September 2026 and have registered for more than fifty competitions since. Four cases are published.

What the pages actually contain

Each case is written as an argument rather than a summary. What the brief asked, what we decided the real question was, what we got wrong, and what we would do differently. The slides are the ones actually submitted, shown at a size where you can read the numbers on them, not redrawn.

Where a chart appears that is not a slide, it was rebuilt from the team's own figures so it could be read on a phone and by a screen reader. Those are labelled as ours. The slides are labelled as theirs.

On the briefs

A competition brief belongs to the organiser who wrote it. Unless a case page says otherwise, the brief on it is restated in our own words and everything that follows is our own work. Where an organiser has given permission to reproduce theirs, the page says so and names the permission.

On the numbers

Competition cases are built on assumptions, because the briefs withhold most of the financials on purpose. Every case here states its assumptions rather than burying them, and says plainly which part of the finding is robust and which part rests on a number we chose. Where a figure is a team estimate it is marked as one.

Nothing on this site is a client engagement, and nothing here should be read as advice about a real company. The companies in these briefs are the organisers' inventions.

The two lanes

Case competitions and hackathons are different problems and we do not pretend otherwise. A case is judged on whether the argument survives a panel. A hackathon is judged on whether the thing runs. The work, the preparation and the failure modes have almost nothing in common, so they are kept apart on the record and the roster for each is listed separately.
